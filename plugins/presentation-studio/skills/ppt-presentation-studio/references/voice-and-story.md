# Speaker voice and human writing

## Calibrate before drafting

Ask for one authentic sample when available: a prior deck, email, proposal introduction, message, transcript, or recorded wording. Explain that the purpose is to match how the presenter actually speaks, not to copy confidential material unnecessarily.

If no sample exists, create three title families using the real topic:

1. Direct and executive.
2. Conversational and human.
3. Editorial or argumentative.

Provide two or three titles per family. Ask which sounds most natural, invite a rewrite, and record both selected and rejected language.

## Voice profile

Capture:

- language and regional variant;
- formality;
- directness;
- first-person usage;
- sentence and title length;
- technical depth;
- preferred terms;
- expressions to avoid;
- selected title examples;
- user rewrites.

## Human-writing rules

- Use concrete nouns, verbs, numbers, decisions, and consequences.
- Preserve useful phrases from the presenter.
- Prefer a claim the speaker can defend over a slogan that merely sounds polished.
- Vary sentence rhythm naturally.
- Use regional vocabulary only when it is authentic and respectful.
- Avoid symmetrical card copy that repeats the same sentence pattern mechanically.
- Avoid unnecessary English terminology when a natural local term exists.

Reject generic formulas unless the presenter explicitly prefers them:

- unlocking potential;
- redefining the future;
- activating the conversation;
- a new era of;
- transformative ecosystem;
- journey toward innovation.

## Story discipline

Every deck must answer:

1. Why are we discussing this now?
2. What does the audience need to understand or believe?
3. What evidence changes the decision?
4. What action follows?

Use section beats and contrast, not decorative drama. Titles should carry the argument rather than label a topic whenever that remains natural in the speaker's voice.

## Approval

Show the voice summary and representative titles. Set `approvals.voice` only when the user confirms or supplies a preferred rewrite.
